In simple terms, a **token** is like a **digital placeholder** or a **representation of something else**.

Think of it like this:

*   **In a game:** You might have a plastic token that represents your player on the board. The token itself isn't valuable, but it stands for your character and their progress.
*   **In a casino:** You exchange your real money for casino chips (tokens). You use these chips to play games, and you can exchange them back for real money. The chip is a token for your money.
*   **Online logins:** When you log into a website, you might get a "session token." This token is a temporary digital ticket that proves you're logged in. The website uses this token to recognize you without you having to re-enter your password every time.

**Key ideas to remember about tokens:**

*   **They represent something:** They don't have intrinsic value on their own, but they stand for something else that does (money, access, a piece in a game, a right, etc.).
*   **They can be used for exchange or access:** You can often trade tokens for something else or use them to get into a place or use a service.
*   **They can be digital or physical:** While often used in digital contexts now, the concept applies to physical objects too.

So, whenever you hear "token," think of a small, representative item that stands for something more significant.
